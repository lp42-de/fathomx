Years of runway

Seventy-one percent of this planet is ocean. It receives more sunlight than any desert. Its winds never stop. Its waves carry energy that has traveled thousands of miles undisturbed. And it belongs to no one.

Every great energy infrastructure project in history has been constrained by borders — by who owns the land, who controls the permits, who can veto the future. The high seas have none of those constraints. FathomX is the first company building there.

Three sources.
One platform.

A floating structure anchored in international waters captures energy from every direction — sun overhead, wind across the deck, waves below the hull. Each source is modular. The platform is permanent.

Energy sources
☀

Solar

The open ocean receives some of the highest solar irradiance on Earth, unobstructed by terrain or weather patterns that limit land-based installations. Marine-grade panels run continuously without the permitting battles that plague onshore development.

◎

Wind

The trade winds and monsoon corridors that sailors have relied on for centuries are the most consistent wind resources on the planet. Floating turbines — proven commercially since 2017 — capture them far from any coastline claim.

〜

Wave

Swell that has crossed entire ocean basins arrives at the platform carrying mechanical energy. Wave converters integrated into the mooring system turn that motion into power — a source that doesn't stop when the sun sets or the wind eases.

What we do with it
⚡

Power to the mainland

A submarine cable carries energy from the platform directly to a national grid. The technology exists today — the same cables that already link countries across the North Sea. We use them to cross a different kind of border.

◻

Compute at sea

The same cable that carries energy outbound carries compute capacity inbound. Data infrastructure running on clean, unrestricted offshore power — available to anyone, sitting under no government's jurisdiction.

◇

Food from open water

The exclusion zone around the platform becomes a de facto marine reserve. Offshore aquaculture — powered by the platform, staffed by the crew — produces a third export stream alongside energy and compute.

Briefly.

Every category of energy infrastructure on land has incumbents, regulations, and entrenched interests. Offshore international waters has none of those things yet. The legal framework is forming. The technology is ready. The first company to transmit power from international waters to a mainland grid will own a position that cannot be taken.

That company needs to exist before the window closes. FathomX is building it now.

The technology is here.

Floating wind farms operate commercially. Submarine cables carry a gigawatt across 700 kilometers. Subsea hardware runs cooler and more reliably than anything on land. The pieces exist. Nobody has assembled them in international waters.

The world needs what we're building.

Nations that import energy pay a sovereignty premium every year. Island grids run on diesel at prices that would be unthinkable on the mainland. A clean, independent offshore energy source with no geopolitical strings changes that equation permanently.

This is a 40-year asset, not a product.

A platform anchored in international waters doesn't depreciate the way a startup does. It generates revenue on a 25-year power purchase agreement, with three independent income streams, and no landlord who can evict you.
