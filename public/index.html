The North Sea, the Persian Gulf, the Bass Strait — every offshore energy project in history has been built inside someone's waters. The open ocean has never been used to generate energy at scale. Not because it can't be. Because nobody has gone there yet.

Why this.
Why now.

01

Every piece already exists.

Floating turbines operate commercially off Norway. Submarine cables carry a gigawatt 720 kilometers across the North Sea. Offshore structures survive Category 5 hurricanes. The components are proven. Nobody has assembled them beyond the 200-mile line.

02

Half the world pays too much for power.

Island nations run diesel generators at $0.40/kWh. Fast-growing economies import energy from neighbors they don't fully trust. A 25-year supply agreement with no geopolitical strings changes the calculation permanently.

03

The ocean is the moat.

A platform in international waters can't be zoned out, eminent-domained, or have its license pulled after an election. The competitive position is geographic. The ocean isn't going anywhere. The asset life is forty years.

Energy, delivered.
